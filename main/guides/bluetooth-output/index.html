    Bluetooth Output¶

    An ADSBee 1090 can stream traffic to one app over Bluetooth Low Energy, as GDL90 (the same heartbeat, ownship, traffic and FIS-B it sends to WiFi access point clients on UDP port 4000) or as FLARM NMEA for gliding apps. The tablet stays on its own network for internet. Off by default.

    Turn on¶

    Send on the CLI:

    AT+BLE_OUTPUT=GDL90
AT+SETTINGS=SAVE

    Or use Settings → Reporting → Bluetooth Output on the device's webpage.

    Command Does
    AT+BLE_OUTPUT=<mode> OFF, GDL90 or FLARM_NMEA
    AT+BLE_OUTPUT? Shows the mode and its state: OFF, ADVERTISING (waiting for an app), CONNECTED, STREAMING, BLOCKED_BY_WIFI, BLOCKED_BY_REMOTE_ID, BLOCKED_BY_RAM or NOT_IN_BUILD

    The ADSBee advertises as ADSBee 1090 XXXX (the end of its Bluetooth MAC address). In the app, pick it from the Bluetooth or external device list. Apps that pair first (AvareX) pair with no PIN.

    Apps¶

    GDL90 over Bluetooth works with apps that read GDL90 from a generic Bluetooth LE serial device:

    App Platform Works
    AvareX Android Yes. Pair the ADSBee in AvareX's IO screen, then connect.
    XCSoar 7.45 and newer Android Yes, with the GDL90 driver on a Bluetooth LE port.
    ForeFlight, Garmin Pilot, FlyQ, WingX, iFly, OzRunways, AvPlan, EasyVFR, SkyDemon iOS, Android No. Use WiFi (UDP port 4000).

    SkyDemon, SeeYou Navigator and Enroute read Bluetooth traffic as FLARM NMEA: use FLARM_NMEA.

    FLARM NMEA¶

    AT+BLE_OUTPUT=FLARM_NMEA sends FLARM data port sentences (FLARM FTD-012, data port version 9) once per second:

    Sentence Sent Content
    GPRMC, GPGGA Ownship known and UTC time known (GNSS, or NTP) Ownship position, speed, track; GNSS altitude (mean sea level)
    PGRMZ Ownship from its own ADS-B Ownship pressure altitude
    PFLAU Always Heartbeat: number of aircraft received, GPS state. TX is 0 and there are no alarms
    PFLAA Ownship known, aircraft altitude comparable (below) One per aircraft, nearest first: position relative to ownship, track, speed, climb, aircraft type, source (ADS-B, ADS-R, TIS-B), signal strength

    FLARM positions are relative to ownship, so traffic needs ownship from AT+RX_POSITION source GNSS (a GNSS fix) or ICAO (the tracked aircraft with your own ICAO address). A fixed position or the lowest aircraft is not ownship. Without ownship only PFLAU goes out, with GPS 0; apps show the device as connected without GPS.

    Vertical separation only compares altitudes with the same reference: pressure altitude against pressure altitude, else geometric (GNSS) altitude against geometric altitude. Pressure and GNSS altitudes differ with the weather, so they are never subtracted from each other. An ICAO ownship has its own pressure altitude and, once its ADS-B velocity messages have given its GNSS minus pressure difference, a geometric one too; so has most traffic. A GNSS fix gives an altitude above mean sea level, while ADS-B and Remote ID geometric altitudes are above the WGS84 ellipsoid (they differ by the local geoid height, up to about 100 m), so a GNSS ownship has no altitude to compare traffic with yet and sends no PFLAA. Aircraft without an altitude in one of ownship's references are counted in PFLAU but get no PFLAA (FTD-012 has no "unknown" vertical, and XCSoar drops a PFLAA without one).

    Horizontal positions are the distance along the WGS84 ellipsoid split into north and east by the direction from ownship, within 1 m of the exact geodesic out to 20 km, and computed in integer arithmetic.

    ADSBee receives only: it doesn't transmit FLARM and computes no collision alarms. Every alarm level is 0 and PFLAU reports TX 0, which some apps show as "No FLARM transmission".

    The stream uses the same Bluetooth LE profile as FLARM's own devices (service FFE0, characteristic FFE1), so apps set up the way FLARM's EFB guide (FTD-115) describes should read it. Pick ADSBee 1090 XXXX where the guide names the FLARM device.

    App Setup Checked
    XCSoar Device port: ADSBee 1090 XXXX (BLE sensor), driver FLARM XCSoar's NMEA parser reads the stream; not tried in the app
    Enroute New connection → Bluetooth → ADSBee 1090 XXXX Enroute's FLARM parser reads the stream; not tried in the app
    SkyDemon Connectivity → Add Bluetooth Device → ADSBee 1090 XXXX → NMEA (FLARM or GPS) Not tested
    SeeYou Navigator Devices → add ADSBee 1090 XXXX; enable the FLARM layer Not tested
    LK8000, XCTrack Not tested

    Profiles¶

    Both generic serial profiles are offered; the app subscribes to one.

    Service Data characteristic
    Nordic UART Service 6E400001-B5A3-F393-E0A9-E50E24DCCA9E Notify 6E400003-…; writes to 6E400002-… are ignored
    HM-10 / TI CC254x FFE0 FFE1: notify; writes are ignored

    Data is the GDL90 byte stream, framed as on UDP (0x7E flags, byte stuffing, CRC), or NMEA sentences ending in CR LF. A notification holds whole messages or sentences unless one is longer than the notification.

    Limits¶

    • One app at a time.
    • ADSBees without PSRAM (AT+DEVICE_INFO? shows ESP32 PSRAM: NONE) need the WiFi access point and station off (BLOCKED_BY_WIFI). Ethernet can stay on.
    • Without PSRAM, Remote ID over Bluetooth takes priority: the output waits (BLOCKED_BY_REMOTE_ID) while Remote ID receive or transmit uses Bluetooth. With PSRAM both run.
    • Throughput: notifications carry 244 Bytes. With PSRAM, about 180 of the 200 aircraft the ESP32 tracks go out every second (about 6 kB/s). Without PSRAM, about 95 aircraft go out per second; with more, the next report starts where the last one stopped, so each aircraft still goes out every few seconds. The heartbeat always goes out first, and FIS-B uplinks are dropped before traffic.
