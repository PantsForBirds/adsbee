  • The RF front end is fine. Two BLB01s and two TFS1090Fs give ≈0.78 dB NF and 33.6 dB gain (est.). That is about 4.7 dB better noise than the m1421's LR2021. The gap is not in the RF front end, so LNA and detector swaps cannot close it.
  • The dB go after the detector. Against a matched-filter receiver with the same NF (-100.5 dBm), the model loses 2.6 dB to the 23.7 MHz SAW bandwidth, 1.7 dB to the 1-bit slicer and demod, and 4.4 dB to the PIO preamble matcher. The matcher needs the TL well above the noise; the data bits would prefer it lower.
  • The default TL offset (600 mV) is too low for the matcher. It puts the threshold 55 mV (3 dB) above the mean log noise. Live-sky captures show the comparator HI 14.0 % of the time on noise; the model gives 14.5 %. At that duty, noise edges keep the 4 round-robin matchers busy, and the model misses about 16 % of frames at every level, strong ones included. At 800 mV it reaches -91.8 dBm.
  • The bench −84 dBm is probably a bench artifact. The bench TL readback (1.39–1.49 V at offset 600) implies a noise floor 2.6–8 dB above thermal. An in-band CW at −95 dBm (likely Pluto LO leakage at 1090 MHz) reproduces both that readback (0.875 V) and the bench sensitivity (-85.2 dBm modeled vs −83.6 measured). In thermal noise the same model puts the 1090U at -91.8 dBm, ahead of the m1421 model (-90.2 dBm; m1421 spec −89).
  • Recommendation. Do: confirm the bench leak (M1, 1 hour) before trusting any sensitivity number, m1421 included. Do: A/B the TL offset 600 vs 800 mV on air (free; model: the 90 % point appears at -91.8 dBm, decode rate above threshold 84 % → 97 % (model)). Maybe: C17 3 → 22 pF with the TL relearned (+2.3 dB model, one cap). Maybe: TA3003 as X2 (+1.2 dB alone, +3.2 dB with C17; not stocked). Don't: LNA, detector or comparator swaps.

AD8313 output drivesources 400 µA, sinks 10 mA. R12 (1 kΩ to 3V3) supplies ≈2.5 mA, so the rising edge is not current-limited. Loads: R11, R13, R14 ≥ 5.1 kΩ.OK

The video path is 3–6× wider than Mode S needs, so the slicer sees about 58 mV rms of noise on FAST (model; AD8313 out 66 mV rms) against a 55 mV TL step at default settings. Narrowing it with C17 is the cheapest sensitivity change found (section 5). The cost is slower edges and more amplitude-dependent delay on the capture timestamp: with 22 pF (τ 112 ns) a weak and a strong frame can trigger up to ~100 ns apart (est.), about 30 m in MLAT. Check this before shipping; 10 pF halves it.

4. Comparator, trigger level and the preamble matchers

  • TL network. LEVEL = (DET·R9 + LEVEL_BIAS·R13)/(R9+R13) on 200 pF (τ 1.8 µs). It tracks the detector mean, so the setting only adds offset/11: 600 mV → 54.5 mV ≈ 3.1 dB above the mean log noise. During a frame LEVEL rises toward mid-pulse, which is why data bits survive a low TL.
  • TL ripple. PWM 25 kHz into 10 kΩ/100 nF: ≈31 mV p-p on LEVEL_BIAS, 2.8 mV p-p on LEVEL (est.). Noise on LEVEL is 8 mV rms (model). Ripple is negligible.
  • Comparator. Hysteresis 1–5 mV (0.06–0.3 dB), negligible. Offset ±3 mV typ, ±10 mV max = ±33 / ±110 mV of TL setting. That moves each unit's optimum TL. A unit-level TL learn matters more than any comparator swap. A faster push-pull part (TLV3601) changed nothing in the model.
  • Noise duty is measured, not assumed. The comparator HI fraction in noise-only tails of 4000 live-sky captures (t-0870, attic 1090U) is 14.0 %. The model gives 14.5 % at TL 600, with the same run-length shape: 76 % of HI runs last one sample (83 ns) in both. The attic unit's TL offset was not logged; the default is assumed.
  • Why a low TL hurts. At ≈1.2 noise edges/µs, each candidate holds a matcher for 1.3–2.3 µs before it spends its 4-mismatch budget. The token passes strictly in ring order (wait 1 irq 4 rel). If the matcher whose turn it is is still busy when a frame starts, it next waits for a falling edge and takes pulse 2 or later, misaligned. The frame is lost at any level.

Layout and next revision

  • Self-interference check first (M2). U2/X1/U3/X2 sit on the top side directly over the ESP32-S3-MINI-1U (bottom side). Clock harmonics in or near the SAW passband: 12 MHz × 91 = 1092 MHz (RP2040 Y1), 40 MHz × 27 = 1080 MHz (ESP32; X1+X2 pass it at ≈−1.7 dB), 48 MHz × 23 = 1104 MHz (CC1312 Y2, ≈−13 dB). A spur 5 dB over thermal noise would cost several dB in the same way as the bench CW. If M2 finds one: a shield can over U2–X2 and stitched ground between the RF strip and the module on rev H.
  • Rev H options if the model holds on air: C17 footprint value 10–22 pF; L6 7.5 nH; a narrow SAW at X2 if one can be bought; keep D7 (harmless).
  • Bigger lever, not RF: a soft preamble correlator on the 12 Msps sample stream would recover most of the 4.4 dB between -91.8 and the perfect-timing -96.2 dBm. Simple PIO rule changes (budget 8, per-chip majority) gave ≤0.5 dB in the model.

6. Bench measurements to confirm (proposed, not run)

pi-1 is busy: these are for whoever next has a quiet bench. Order is by value.

  1. M1 · Bench CW leak (1 h). Waggler idle with its TX still enabled at 1090 MHz. (a) Read AT+TL_READ? 10× and average. (b) Unplug the bench feed at the 1090U and fit a 50 Ω U.FL terminator; read again. (c) Expected for thermal noise: TL_READ ≈ 0.752 V + offset (≈1.35 V at 600). A drop of more than 20 mV with the terminator means the feed carries in-band power. (d) Confirm on a spectrum view: feed the bench cable into the second Pluto's RX at 1090 MHz, 20 MSps, and compare idle-TX vs TX-disabled. (e) Re-run the t-0827 DF17 sweep with the waggler LO at 1110 MHz and frames synthesized at −20 MHz offset; X1+X2 then reject the leak by >40 dB. Do the same for the m1421.
  2. M2 · Terminated noise floor and self-interference (1 h). 50 Ω on J1, bias tee off. Log AT+TL_READ? (minus offset) for each case: ESP32 Wi-Fi off, Wi-Fi on with traffic, Ethernet/feeds active, CC1312 RX on. Expected ≈0.752 V; every 17.6 mV above is 1 dB of excess noise. Then look at TJ1 (MM8930 test port; the switch disconnects the detector) with the Pluto RX: 1070–1110 MHz, RBW ≈10 kHz, for lines at 1080, 1092 and 1104 MHz.
  3. M3 · Noise figure, Y-factor through the detector (1 h, needs a noise source). Noise source (ENR 5–15 dB, e.g. a 346B or a calibrated diode) on J1, firmware noise floor (RSSI, mV) read with the source off and on, 64 averages each. Y(dB) = ΔV / 17.6 mV. NF = ENR − 10·log10(10^(Y/10) − 1). Expected NF ≈ 0.78 dB, Y ≈ ENR. Pluto-only alternative: measure the noise power at TJ1 into the Pluto RX with the source off/on (backend NF is diluted by 33 dB of gain).
  4. M4 · Comparator duty and edge rate vs TL (30 min). Antenna on, then terminated. TL offset 400–1200 mV in 100 mV steps. Logic analyzer on TP4 (PULSES) at ≥50 MSps, 1 s per step: HI fraction and rising edges per second. Compare with the model curve above (14.5 % / ≈1.2 edges/µs at 600, 7 % at 800). This tests the mechanism behind the TL recommendation directly.
  5. M5 · TL ripple and detector noise (30 min). Scope, 10× probe, 20 MHz BW limit, short ground spring. TP1 (LEVEL_BIAS) AC: ≈31 mV p-p 25 kHz triangle. TP5 (LEVEL) AC: ≈2.8 mV p-p ripple under ≈8 mV rms noise. TP3 (FAST) AC: ≈58 mV rms; TP2 (DET) ≈66 mV rms (model). Anything much larger points to pickup on the TL network.
  6. M6 · Detector match (30 min, VNA or Pluto + bridge). S11 into TJ1 toward the detector, 0.8–1.3 GHz. Expected resonance 0.9–0.96 GHz, RL 2–7 dB at 1090 MHz. Retest with L6 = 7.5 nH.
  7. M7 · A/B on air (2 days, no lab time). Same unit, alternating 30-min blocks of TL 600 / 800 (and 22 pF C17 at 400 / 500 on a reworked unit), as in t-0870: frames/s per aircraft and DF17 position rate per strong aircraft (expected 2.0/s). The model predicts the decode rate of frames above threshold going from 84 % to 97 %, strong aircraft included.

7. Method and confidence

  • Model. Complex baseband at 96 MHz: thermal noise at NF 0.78 dB → TFS1090F×2 response (t-0061 extraction) → log of envelope power with the firmware calibration and AD8313 noise → AD8313 2-pole video (40 ns rise) → FAST/LEVEL network with D7 (implicit Euler at 384 MHz, 1N4148 fit from the Comchip curve) → MCP6566 with hysteresis and a 10 ns input pole → 4 preamble matchers cycle-exact to capture.pio (48 MHz, round robin, budget 4) → 12 Msps sampler → the Python port of mode_s_soft_demod.cc (worker-2, t-0870) plus the #398 offset retry. No SPICE engine is installed here, so the analog network runs in this ODE model instead of LTspice.
  • Validated against. (1) Live-sky noise duty 14.0 % vs 14.5 % and the run-length shape. (2) Bench −83.6 dBm vs -85.2 dBm with a −95 dBm CW, whose detector floor matches the bench TL readback. (3) m1421 spec −89 dBm vs LR2021 model -90.2 dBm.
  • Not validated. The CW-leak explanation; the matcher-ring loss on real hardware (from reading the PIO, not observed); AD8313 treated as an ideal log-of-envelope with a 2-pole output; linear-phase SAWs (group-delay ripple ignored); carrier offset 0 (bench case); LR2021 NF (5.5 dB est.); pulse shapes; one board revision (F+). On the bench, TL 500 beat TL 600 by 0.7 dB. The model agrees at a −92 dBm CW (-82.8 vs -82.4 dBm) but not at −95 dBm (-83.4 vs -85.2 dBm), so the leak level is uncertain within a few dB.
